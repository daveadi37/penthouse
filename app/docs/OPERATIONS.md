# How the house uses this

For Earl and Rosie. This is not about the software — it is the day, written
down, and where in the app each part of it lives.

The one thing to hold on to: **the running sheet is the day.** Everything else
in the app is a record. The sheet is the instruction.

---

## The 09:00 rhythm

The sheet goes to the **3808 Home** WhatsApp group **by 09:00**. Every morning.

| When | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| The night before, or first thing | Rosie | Opens today's sheet. Fills in the prayer start time, the number of meals, the guests, and the menu as far as it is known. |
| Before 09:00 | Earl | Reads it. Taps **Check**. His name goes on it as the person who checked it. |
| By 09:00 | Whoever prepared it | Taps **Post**, types their own name on the prepared-by line, and copies the exported text into the group. |

The app reminds whoever prepares it before 09:00, and flags the sheet as late
after. The reminder is not a nag — a sheet that goes out at 09:40 has already
missed Marvin's shopping run.

Preparing it the night before is worth doing. It slips most on the days Rosie
is also making the shopping list.

**Earl checks it before it goes out.** That is the whole point of the checked-by
line, and it is printed on every sheet. If Earl has not read it, it is not
ready.

---

## What must never be left blank

> Never send this sheet out with the prayer start time or the number of meals
> left blank.

The app enforces this. It will not let a sheet be posted with either one
missing, and it will tell you which. This is not the app being awkward:

- **The prayer start time** is what everybody's day is built backwards from.
  The set-up has to be finished forty-five minutes before it, Reza's kitchen
  hour and Jagdishbhai's cooking sit in front of it, and the guests are told a
  time based on it.
- **The number of meals** is what Jagdishbhai cooks to and what Hiteshbhai
  makes breads for. Guess it and either people go without or a third of the
  food is thrown away.

If you genuinely do not know the number yet, put your best figure in and
correct it. A figure that gets revised is useful. A blank is not.

The third thing the app asks for is a name on the **prepared by** line. Not a
role — a name. When something on the sheet turns out to be wrong, that is who
knows what was meant.

## The two rules, word for word

Both of these are printed on every single sheet, at the top, above the roster.
They are not reminders that fade after a few days of the observance. They are on
the page every day because they are the two things that cannot be got wrong.

### FOOD RULE — THIS IS NOT OPTIONAL

> All food today is vegetarian. NO meat. NO fish. NO eggs. Milk, cheese, yoghurt
> and butter are fine. Onion and garlic are fine.
>
> Do not bring meat into the kitchen or the home today. Wash shrine items with
> the shrine sponge only — that sponge never touches meat or normal washing-up.

### PRAYER ITEM RULE — THIS IS NOT OPTIONAL

> Any items brought for prayer (Milk, Yoghurt, Juices, Fruits etc) are to be
> marked and not used for Consumption.

In practice, the prayer item rule means Marvin marks the containers as he brings
them in — a marker pen on the lid is enough. Items marked for prayer are flagged
as such in the inventory too, so they do not turn up in a stock count as
available. If a container is not marked, treat it as prayer stock and ask, not
the other way round.

## The shrine

Rosie dusts the shrine, checks the divo and tops it up. That is her duty on the
roster, not a favour, and it comes before the kitchen in the morning.

The seven things checked before prayers, in the app under **Daily checks →
SHRINE — before prayers**:

1. Divo lit and topped up — correct oil only
2. Shoes off before going near the shrine
3. Dusted with the shrine cloth — no sprays, no chemicals
4. Used matchsticks and ash cleared away
5. Statues not moved
6. Area around the shrine clear
7. Matches, wicks and 2 spare bottles of divo oil in stock

Four things about that list are worth saying plainly:

- **The shrine cloth and the shrine sponge are separate items.** They are
  marked as shrine-only in the inventory. The cloth never meets a spray or a
  chemical; the sponge never meets meat or the normal washing-up. If either one
  cannot be found, do not substitute — say so and wait.
- **Statues are not moved.** Not to dust behind, not to make room, not back
  again afterwards.
- **Two spare bottles of divo oil, always.** Not one. The divo burns down over
  about three days and Marvin buys the oil first thing, so one spare is already
  a problem. Log the divo in the app each time it is checked or topped up — when
  the level is logged as low or empty, Earl is told automatically.
- **The divo is checked and topped up before anyone goes to bed.** It is the
  last thing on the close-down list for that reason.

### When you are not sure

> **IF YOU ARE NOT SURE ABOUT THE SHRINE OR THE PRAYERS — STOP AND ASK ADITYA
> BEFORE YOU DO ANYTHING.**

Aditya, for anything to do with the shrine, the prayers, the priests or the
prayer items. He stays with the priests all day and knows what they have asked
for.

Earl, for anything else that is unclear — the sheet, the timings, the staff, the
guests, money.

Stopping and asking costs a few minutes. Guessing at the shrine cannot be
undone.

## A day with no prayers

It happens — between observances, or on a day the priests are not sitting.

- Open the sheet as normal. Leave the occasion line and the prayer times blank.
- **Do not post it.** A sheet with no prayer start time cannot be posted, and
  should not be — there is nothing on it for the group to act on. Send one line
  to the group instead: no prayers today, and what the meal is.
- The shrine group of checks still applies in full. The divo is still lit,
  still topped up, still checked before bed. Nothing about the shrine depends on
  whether there are prayers.
- The food rule still applies on any day the priests are in the home or prayer
  items are in the kitchen. If you are unsure whether today counts, ask Aditya.
- Write the reason in the sheet's notes. In a month's time nobody will remember
  why there is a gap, and the notes are the only place that answer lives.

## A new day of the observance

The occasion line — "9th day of the Prayer" — counts the days of the observance
for you. It is worked out from the observance's start date, so it is right
without anyone typing it.

When the date rolls over, opening the sheet for the new day gives you a fresh
one. What carries over and what does not:

| Carries over | Starts fresh |
|---|---|
| The roster — all eight rows, with their hours and duties | The menu — six blank lines |
| The order of the day, every row | The guests |
| The standing shopping rows — daily milk and yoghurt, divo oil, flowers and incense | The thirty-one checks, all unticked |
| The occasion line, advanced by one day | The meals and guest counts |
| The two rules and the footer rules | The prayer times |

The menu and the guests deliberately do not carry over. Yesterday's menu
reappearing as today's is how a wrong dish gets cooked, and an old guest list is
worse than none.

Ticks do not carry over either. A tick means somebody looked, today.

If a whole observance is starting or finishing, that is a change to the
observance itself rather than to a sheet — Earl sets the start date, the end
date and the number of days once, and every sheet in between names itself.

## Adding a guest, a dish, a shopping line

All three work the same way. Find the section, tap **Add row**, fill it in, save.

**A guest** — section 5. Name, when they are arriving, and the note. The note is
the part that matters: what they cannot eat, and where they sit. "Both need
chairs, not floor seating. No chilli." is a useful note. "VIP" is not. Use "-"
for the arrival time if nobody knows yet, and update it when you do.

Every guest added changes the meals count and the number of mats and chairs laid
out. Change the header count at the same time, or the sheet contradicts itself.

**A dish** — section 3. Dish, who makes it, how many, notes. Who makes it should
be a person, not "kitchen" — Jagdishbhai, Hiteshbhai and Rosie divide the menu
between them and the column is how each of them knows what is theirs. Aditya
agrees the menu with the cooks in advance, so a dish added on the day should be
mentioned to him.

**A shopping line** — section 4. Item, how much, in stock, who buys. The
in-stock column has four states and *partial* is a real answer — one spare
bottle of divo oil when two are wanted is partial, not yes. Mark it partial and
write what is missing in the notes.

Jagdishbhai hands Rosie next week's shopping list when he leaves at 17:00, and
confirms that the stock for tomorrow's meal is complete before he goes. Put that
list into section 4 the same evening while it is in front of you.

## Who ticks which group, and when

Four groups, thirty-one boxes. A tick records who and when, so tick your own —
ticking on somebody else's behalf makes the record useless.

| Group | Who ticks it | When |
|---|---|---|
| **SHRINE — before prayers** (7) | Rosie | Morning, before the kitchen. Well before the set-up. |
| **PRAYER SET-UP — finished by 15:45** (8) | Marvin, Earl, Aditya | From about 14:15. All eight ticked by 15:45, not at 15:44. |
| **THE HOUSE — before the first guest arrives** (7) | Rosie and Reza | From 15:00, as Reza comes off the kitchen. |
| **AFTER THE MEAL — close-down** (9) | Reza and Rosie | After the meal, before Reza's shift ends at 22:00. |

The set-up group has a hard deadline for a reason. **Mics on and tested** means
somebody spoke into them and somebody else listened from the back. Finding out
at 16:00 is finding out too late.

Three of the boxes are easy to tick without doing:

- **Ken shut away from the prayer area and the open doors.** He is a cat and the
  balcony doors are open all evening. Marvin checks him during prayers as well,
  not only before.
- **Guest toilet spotless and stocked.** Reza then checks it **every 20 minutes**
  while the prayers run, and logs each check in the app. The log is what shows
  the round actually held up on a busy sitting.
- **Photos of the set-up and the clear-up posted on the 3808 Home group.** Two
  photographs, both posted. Attach them to the sheet as well so they are still
  findable in six months, when WhatsApp has lost them.

### During the prayers

Not a check group, but the same discipline. The prayers run from the start time
until about 19:30–20:00 with **2 or 3 breaks**, and **water goes round to
everyone at every break**. Start and end each break in the app and say whether
the water went round. If it did not, the app says to tell Earl, and it means it.

Aditya tells Marvin what time the prayers will actually finish, so Hiteshbhai
can time the breads. Log the actual finish time on the sheet when you know it.

And: **no messages passed second-hand.** Earl keeps Marvin informed directly.
A message relayed through a third person arrives late and slightly wrong.

## When something is broken, missing or missed

> Anything broken, missing or missed — tell Earl the same day.

Same day. Not at the end of the week, and not only if it turns out to matter.

Raise it in the app under **Issues** — a photograph first, then the area, then
one line about what is wrong. It reaches Earl straight away. A photograph and
one honest line beats a careful paragraph written the next morning.

What counts:

- Something physically broken or damaged, however small.
- Something missing — and the shrine items and the divo oil count twice over.
  A missing shrine sponge stops the close-down.
- Something on the sheet that did not happen. A box left unticked is not a
  failure to record, it is a thing that did not get done, and Earl needs to know
  which.
- A break where the water did not go round. A 20-minute toilet round that
  slipped. A guest whose dietary note was not honoured.
- Money spent. **Cash spent is logged the same evening, with the receipt.**
  Photograph the receipt into the app; the paper goes wherever the paper goes,
  but the photograph is the record.

Nothing on this list gets anyone into trouble for reporting it. Not reporting it
is the thing that does.

## One-line summary of the day

Marvin buys the regular items in the morning. Rosie lists anything else needed
at 09:00 and the sheet is posted to the group by then. Reza comes in at 14:00,
Jagdishbhai at 15:00. Set-up finished by 15:45. Prayers begin at 16:00 and run
to about 19:30 with two or three breaks, water round at every one.
Jagdishbhai leaves at 17:00, handing his food and next week's list to Rosie.
Hiteshbhai comes at 19:00 for the reheating and the breads. Aarti, then prasad,
then the meal. Hiteshbhai leaves at 21:00, Reza at 22:00. Daily checks after the
meal. Divo topped up, photographs posted and cash logged before bed.
