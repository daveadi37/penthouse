# How the house uses this

Written for Earl and for whoever is holding the phone, not for a developer.

The one thing to hold on to: **the buy list is the point.** Everything else in
here supports it or sits beside it. If the buy list is right, the house does not
run out of things, and that is most of the job.

---

## The rhythm

| When | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| As she uses things | Rosie | Counts down what she opens. Not at the end — when she takes the last one out of the cupboard. |
| Sunday | Rosie | Counts the cupboard properly. Twenty minutes with the phone. |
| Before the shop | Marvin | Opens the buy list, taps **Start the shop**. |
| At the till | Marvin | Ticks things in as they go in the trolley, puts the price in, photographs the receipt. |
| Any time | Anyone | Posts to the house chat if something needs saying. |
| When something breaks | Anyone | Reports it with a photograph. |

Nobody has to remember to build a list. Anything that falls below its minimum is
on it already.

---

## Counting

**Inventory → Count**, or the **Count the cupboard** button at the top.

One screen, one list, big buttons either side of the number. The things that are
low come first, so if Rosie only has five minutes she has counted the five minutes
that mattered.

The number is also a box. If she can see there are seven, she types seven rather
than tapping plus four times.

**Count what you opened, not what you finished.** The minimum on each item is set
at roughly a week's use, because the grocery run is weekly. Counting down the
moment the last packet comes out of the cupboard gives the list a week to catch
it. Counting when it runs out gives it none.

## The buy list

**Inventory → Buy list.** Two kinds of thing land on it:

- **Automatically**, when stock falls below its minimum. Nobody adds these.
- **By hand**, for anything not tracked as stock — a birthday cake, a bulb, paneer
  for Thursday.

**Add them all** puts everything currently below its minimum onto the list in one
tap. That is the button to use on a Sunday after counting.

**Paste a list** takes a list as it is actually written. One thing per line,
quantities where they are there and not where they are not:

```
Basmati rice 5kg
5 x tinned tomatoes
2kg atta
milk
Toilet roll - 12
```

It shows what it matched against existing stock and what it would create new,
and nothing is committed until that looks right.

**Copy the list** puts it on the clipboard as plain text, for pasting into a
message.

### At the shop

**Start the shop** groups the list by where things live, so one pass of the shop
does it. Tick as things go in the trolley; the total at the top goes up as you do.

Put the price in as you tick. It is thirty seconds at the till and it is the
difference between knowing what the house spends and guessing. Photograph the
receipt on the last item.

**Finish** marks everything bought, puts it into stock, and writes the spending
against the right budget. Cat food goes to the cat budget without anyone choosing
it.

## Budgets

The strip at the top of the buy list shows this month's grocery, cleaning and cat
budgets and what is left. It is there and not in the Money screen on purpose —
the useful moment to know you are near the limit is before the shop, not at the
end of the month.

Money itself is Earl's and Aditya's. Rosie and Marvin do not see salaries, staff
records, or anything marked owner-only, and that is enforced by the database
rather than by hiding a menu.

---

## The house chat

**House Chat** is one thread and everybody is in it. Anything the house needs to
know goes there: what is running low, who is coming, what broke, what time the car
is back.

**Pinned notices** stay at the top until somebody takes them down. Use one for
anything that must not scroll away — Shrien's flight, a change to the week, a
contractor coming. Earl and Aditya can pin; anyone can read.

It is not a replacement for reporting a fault. A message scrolls; an issue has a
state and somebody's name on it.

## When something breaks

**Issues → Report** — or the button on the Today screen.

Photograph first. One clear picture saves a long explanation, and it is what the
person fixing it actually wants. Say where it is and what happens. Set the
urgency honestly: **urgent** means water, electricity, safety, or Ken.

Every report has somebody's name on it and a state. Tell Earl the same day if
something is broken, missing or was missed — the app records it, but the app is
not a substitute for saying so.

---

## Dietary

Aditya is vegetarian — one vegetarian portion. That is a note on his record, not a
rule over the kitchen, and the menu planner shows it when portions are set. The
house is not vegetarian.

Anybody else's requirements go the same way: **Roles & Logins → the person →
Dietary needs**. Free text, read by whoever is cooking.

---

## Accounts

Everybody has their own login. Nobody shares one — not because of secrecy, but
because every tick, count and message has a name against it, and a shared account
makes the record useless.

Passwords are set by Aditya, in **Roles & Logins**. There is no reset email,
because the addresses are house addresses rather than real mailboxes. Forgotten
password is a thirty-second job for him, not a lockout.

**What each person sees** is a set of capabilities on their role, not a fixed
list. If Rosie needs to see something she cannot, that is one tick in Roles &
Logins, not a change to the app.

---

## When the wifi drops

Keep working. Ticks, counts and messages are held on the phone and go across when
the signal comes back — the lift is the usual culprit and it is expected.

The line at the foot of the sidebar says which state you are in. If it lists
something under **Not saved**, that change was refused because the person's role
does not allow it, and it says which one. That is the only case where something
is genuinely lost, and it says so rather than pretending.

## What is not in here yet

Honest list, so nobody goes looking:

- **Push notifications do not work, and are further off than they look.** The
  sender and the queue exist; nothing subscribes a device, there is no button to
  turn them on, and the queue is never drained. Notifications are raised and read
  inside the app. Nothing reaches a phone.
- **Meal plans** are Aditya's to supply. The planner works; the plans are not in
  it yet.
- Some screens — the register, documents, guests, staff records — still save to
  the device rather than being shared between phones. They work; two people just
  will not see each other's edits yet. The buy list, stock, the chat and issues
  are shared.
